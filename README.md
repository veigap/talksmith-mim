# How we see the future of knowledge workers

We don't believe in replacement — we believe in amplification: as agents take on more of the execution, humans gain more agency — more room to direct the work, decide, and own the results. This view draws on several recent studies on AI and work, including the **2026 Work Trend Index** by Microsoft [1]. Four ideas that run through the course:

1. **AI expands who can do high-value work.** 66% of AI users now spend more time on high-value work, and 58% produce things they couldn't a year ago.
2. **Human judgment becomes more valuable, not less.** What matters most as AI does more work: quality control over its output (50%) and critical thinking (46%). 86% treat AI output as a starting point, not a final answer.
3. **The manager is the lever.** When a manager actively models AI use, their team gains +17 points in perceived value, +22 in critical thinking, +30 in trust. Organizational factors weigh more than twice as much (67%) as individual mindset (32%).
4. **The most advanced users show the pattern to follow.** They use agents in multi-step workflows, routinely redesign processes, and take part in repeatable team-level practices — they don't just use a tool, they redesign their own work around it.

## What kind of work do leaders actually delegate?

Across enterprise adoption surveys, the same pattern shows up: leaders hand off high-volume, well-defined, language-heavy work first — not judgment calls [2].

| Task | Share of companies delegating it |
| :-- | :-: |
| Data management & extraction | 47% |
| Document analysis & summarization | 41% |
| Report generation | 36% |
| Research synthesis | — |
| Meeting summarization | — |

These tasks share what makes them safe to delegate: high volume, clear structure, mostly-text inputs and outputs. There's a deeper pattern too — **delegation tends to follow repetition**. The goal isn't doing a task once with an agent's help; it's describing it **declaratively** (a Skill, an Instruction) so it repeats on its own, without re-explaining it each time. "Automate" and "delegate" are often the same move in disguise. And the dominant style stays **human-in-the-loop** (38% of companies): execution is delegated, but approval gates and review stay. Speed doesn't have to cost control — the two can grow together.

# Objective

This course starts from the amplification thesis above and makes it concrete: the tasks leaders already delegate — data work, document analysis, reporting, research, meeting follow-ups — are the same ones this course teaches you to hand off well, repeatably, and with a human still in the loop. For managers, AI and agents are quickly becoming the most powerful lever available to improve judgment, speed, and execution, both at the company level and in their own day-to-day work.

This course takes a hands-on approach. You will work directly with state-of-the-art tools that leaders are already using to augment their teams and their decisions, and you will discuss the real challenges of adoption: data quality, cultural resistance, ethical trade-offs, and separating hype from value.

By the end of the program, you will leave with a clearer view of where AI creates real business value, a practical toolkit you can apply immediately, and a new way of thinking about your own productivity as an augmented leader.

Because the future will not belong to companies that simply use AI. It will belong to the managers who are augmented by it.

# Appendix — The Atlas Mission (Days 1–2)

**Days 1 and 2 — the Saturday double session — teach Claude Cowork through one running mission: Atlas, your team's virtual market analyst.** Your boss needs a weekly pulse on three companies — **Apple (AAPL), Microsoft (MSFT), and Nvidia (NVDA)** — delivered two ways: an **email in the inbox once a week** and an **always-updated dashboard** (a Live Artifact) he can open whenever he wants. Instead of building that report by hand every Monday, you build Atlas in Cowork to do it for you — without writing a single line of code.

Across Days 1–2 you grow Atlas through **four milestones**, each a working increment that unlocks a new Cowork building block on top of the last. The full brief lives in `mission.md`.

| Milestone | What Atlas achieves | Cowork superpower unlocked |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **1 · 🥚 Atlas is born** | On-demand report: turns the intern's scattered notes into a clean weekly pulse with a fixed format | **Projects · Instructions · Skills** |
| **2 · 🔎 Atlas learns to research** | Autonomous report: gathers news and prices on its own — no more manual copy-paste | **Tools** (web search) · **Connectors** (market data) |
| **3 · 🐣 Atlas works alone** | Ready-to-send report: every Monday it leaves a draft in Gmail, ready to review and send | **Connectors** (Gmail) · **Schedule** |
| **4 · 📣 Atlas delivers the dashboard** | Live dashboard: the `pulso-semanal` Live Artifact refreshes itself with the latest data every week, living in the Live artifacts tab | **Artifacts** (Live Artifacts) · **Skills** |
