## Key claims
- Central thesis ("the new agency equation"): as agents take on more of the execution, humans get more agency — more room to direct work, make calls, own outcomes. The opportunity is for organizations to turn that agency into value.
- Based on: analysis of trillions of anonymized Microsoft 365 productivity signals + survey of 20,000 workers using AI across 10 countries (US, BR, AU, IN, JP, FR, DE, IT, NL, UK), fielded by Edelman Data x Intelligence, Feb 18–Apr 20, 2026.
- Organizational factors (culture, manager support, talent practices) account for **more than 2x** the reported AI impact of individual factors (67% vs. 32%) — random forest permutation importance, cross-validated across three model families (elastic net, random forest, XGBoost; test R² 0.680/0.689/0.690).
- 49% of Microsoft 365 Copilot chat conversations support cognitive work (analyze, solve, evaluate, think creatively); remainder splits: working with people (19%), finding information (15%), producing work (17%). Based on privacy-preserving analysis of 100,000+ Copilot chats.
- 66% of AI users say AI let them spend more time on high-value work; 58% say they're producing work they couldn't have a year ago.
- **Frontier Professionals** — the most advanced AI users — represent 16% of surveyed AI users. 80% of them report the "high-value work" benefit above (vs. 66% overall). They use agents for multi-step workflows and multi-agent systems, routinely redesign workflows, and participate in shared AI standards practices.
  - Definition (methodology): classified only if reporting a combination of (a) advanced use of AI agents for complex/multi-step work, (b) routine workflow redesign, (c) participation in structured, repeatable AI-enabled practices that scale beyond individual use. n = 3,233 of 20,000 AI users. 44% business decision makers / 56% employees. 50% millennials, 23% Gen X, 22% Gen Z, 4% Boomers. More likely in tech (35%) or financial services (12%); roles in IT (36%) or finance/accounting (11%); 53% in companies with 500+ employees.
- Human judgment premium: 50% cite quality control of AI output, 46% cite critical thinking as the top human skills that matter more as AI takes on more work. 86% say they treat AI output as a starting point, not a final answer, and "stay responsible for the thinking."
- Frontier Professionals are more likely to work without AI intentionally to keep skills sharp (43% vs. 30% non-Frontier) and to pause before starting work to decide human vs. AI split (53% vs. 33%).
- "Four modes of working with AI": delegation, collaboration, asking, exploration — a qualitative/illustrative framework (not a data visualization), based on how engaged/frequent AI use is.
- **The Transformation Paradox**: workers are ready, organizations aren't. Five-zone model from the "Organization vs. Employee AI Readiness Index" (n=16,971 with complete data on both axes, Pearson r=0.55 between the two readiness dimensions):
  - Frontier (both high, reinforcing): 19%
  - Blocked Agency (high individual, low organizational): 10%
  - Unclaimed Capacity (low individual, high organizational): 5%
  - Stalled (both low): 16%
  - Emergent (messy middle): 50%
- Only 26% of AI users say their leadership is clearly and consistently aligned on AI. 65% fear falling behind if they don't adapt quickly with AI, yet 45% say it feels safer to focus on current goals than redesign work with AI. Only 13% say they're rewarded for reinvention even if results aren't met.
- Manager modeling matters: separate Microsoft People Science study (1,800 workers globally, July 2025 — 819 leaders, 520 managers, 461 individual contributors) found that when managers actively modeled AI use, employees reported +17 points AI value, +22 points critical thinking about AI use, +30 points trust in agentic AI. Psychological safety around experimentation → up to +20 points AI readiness/value and 1.4x more likely to be high-frequency agentic AI users.
- Frontier Professionals vs. non-Frontier: manager openly uses AI (85% vs. 64%), sets quality standards for AI work (83% vs. 57%), creates space for experimentation (84% vs. 61%), encourages ambitious work redesign (87% vs. 61%). 2x more likely to say they're rewarded for reinvention regardless of outcome (26% vs. 11%).
- Active agents in the Microsoft 365 ecosystem grew 15x year-over-year (18x in large enterprises).
- Frontier Professionals more likely to say teams brainstorm/refine business processes to identify AI opportunities (63% vs. 32%), share AI tips/agents/learnings/mistakes (61% vs. 36%), discuss quality standards for AI-assisted work (54% vs. 29%). More likely to report agent workflows/handoffs/quality standards documented and repeatable at team (26% vs. 19%), function (29% vs. 17%), org level (25% vs. 14%).
- "Building an evaluation infrastructure" — three questions every Frontier Firm must answer: Who reviews agent performance? Who has authority to update agent workflows? How does a local win get captured and scaled? Answering these builds "Owned Intelligence" — institutional know-how that compounds, is unique to the firm, hard to replicate.
- Four roles needed for coordinated reinvention: employees (rearchitect work around intent/review), leaders (redesign processes around outcomes/agent autonomy), IT (build infrastructure for agent operations at scale — agents as managed entities with identities/permissions/policy enforcement/lifecycle management), security (embed monitoring/policy enforcement/auditability; account for data exfiltration, unintended system actions, unauthorized access risks).
- LinkedIn's 2026 Labor Market Report: employers created at least 1.3 million AI-related job opportunities in the past two years (data annotators, AI engineers, forward-deployed engineers) — roles that didn't exist five years ago.

## Definitions and terminology
- **Agency equation**: as agents take on more execution, human agency (room to direct, decide, own outcomes) expands correspondingly.
- **Frontier Firms**: organizations already redesigning the operating model across employees/leaders/organization to capture AI-driven agency; referenced as a companion concept/report ("Three things Frontier Firms understand about AI").
- **Learning System**: an organization where work continuously produces insight and insight continuously reshapes how work gets done — the end-state described for firms that capture and encode signals from agent-driven work into shared routines.
- **Frontier Professionals**: see Key claims definition above — the 16% of surveyed AI users combining advanced agent use, workflow redesign, and scalable shared practices.
- **Transformation Paradox**: the systemic mismatch where individual readiness to work differently with AI outpaces organizational systems (metrics, incentives, norms) that still reward the old way of working.
- **Owned Intelligence**: institutional know-how built from captured agent-performance signals that compounds over time and is hard for competitors to replicate.
- **AI impact** (methodology term): composite of self-reported outcomes — more creative, doing new kinds of work, higher-quality first drafts, improved work ability, collaboration, feeling in control, improved career prospects, doing high-value work, more likely to stay at company because of AI.
